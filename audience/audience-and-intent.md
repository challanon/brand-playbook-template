# Audience and intent

## Purpose and when to load

Define the people the brand serves, the situation they are in, and the progress they seek. Load for strategy, messaging, content planning, search, journeys, and campaign briefs. Do not reduce an audience to demographics alone: a useful segment has a distinct need, context, and decision pattern.

## Segment register

| Segment ID | Audience | Situation / trigger | Job or need | Desired outcome | Priority | Owner / reviewed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AUD-[id] | [specific group] | [what changed or happened] | [functional and emotional need] | [measurable progress] | [primary/secondary/excluded] | [owner/date] |

Use one row only when people share a material need, context, and message. Split a segment when their decision-maker, barrier, outcome, or applicable product differs.

## Audience characteristics

For each priority segment, record only characteristics that change a decision, message, channel, experience, or compliance requirement.

| Characteristic | Questions to answer | Entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role and decision power | Who uses, influences, approves, pays for, or blocks the choice? | [roles] |
| Context | What tools, constraints, life stage, industry, geography, or urgency shape the need? | [context] |
| Current alternative | What do they do today: competitor, spreadsheet, agency, delay, or nothing? | [alternative] |
| Capability and literacy | What domain, digital, language, and accessibility support do they need? | [details] |
| Trust criteria | What proof, authority, privacy, safety, or peer signal is required? | [details] |
| Purchase conditions | Budget, procurement, timing, risk, contract, or approval constraints? | [details] |
| Channels and formats | Where do they research and which formats are practical? | [channels/formats] |
| Exclusions | Who is not served or should not be targeted? Why? | [boundary] |

Do not record protected or sensitive personal data unless it is necessary, lawful, approved, and handled under governance/data-handling.md.

## Needs, objections, and intent

| Segment | Need / question in their words | Barrier or objection | Proof needed | Intent stage | Preferred format | Desired action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AUD-[id] | [plain-language question] | [risk, cost, effort, doubt] | [evidence type] | [discover/learn/compare/decide/use/support] | [guide/demo/calculator/case study] | [action] |

Write the need as a real question or job, not a company objective. Distinguish search intent from funnel labels: someone can be ready to buy but need implementation evidence before acting.

## Research and evidence

- Start with first-party interviews, support conversations, sales notes, search queries, product research, and behavioural data; record source and date.
- Triangulate a conclusion from more than one signal when it will drive positioning or investment.
- Mark assumptions as hypotheses and link their validation to EXP-*; do not present them as audience facts.
- Useful methods: jobs-to-be-done interviews, journey mapping, query analysis, usability research, and support-ticket themes. Study the GOV.UK user-needs approach (https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) and the brandbook.md persona example (https://github.com/brandbook-md/brandbook.md/blob/main/examples/lumen/audience/personas.md).

## Agent rules

- May adapt a message to a recorded segment and intent.
- Must not invent motivations, demographics, barriers, or a new segment from a single anecdote.
- Escalate contradictory evidence, sensitive segmentation, an excluded audience requesting service, or a target-market change.

## Completion check

- [ ] Every primary segment has a trigger, need, outcome, barrier, proof need, and desired action.
- [ ] Decision roles and excluded audiences are explicit where relevant.
- [ ] Material statements have an owner, source, and review date.
- [ ] Search terms and content ideas map to a real audience question.
